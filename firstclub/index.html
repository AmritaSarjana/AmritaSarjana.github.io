07 · The route

Ten nodes. Ten product questions.

The middle mile moves inventory between nodes. This one moves the shipment from my work into FirstClub's problem. For each node: the question I'd want answered, and the kind of system I'd build to answer it. These are hypotheses about how I'd think, not a description of FirstClub's current systems.

NodeThe product questionWhat I'd build to answer it
BuyingHow do we decide what should exist in the network?Demand forecasting by SKU × Club House × day, feeding a replenishment engine that drafts purchase orders for buyers to approve.
Collection / QCHow do we make quality measurable before it enters the system?Grading captured as structured data on every lot (grade, ripeness, expiry), so freshness travels with the inventory.
DCHow do we turn physical inventory into reliable system inventory?One inventory source of truth, updated by events (receive, putaway, pick, adjust) rather than end-of-day syncs.
Middle mileHow do we move the right inventory at the right time?Allocation and trip planning driven by Club House demand and stock age, not fixed schedules.
Club HouseHow do we manage freshness, storage, picking and waste?FEFO picking and ageing alerts in frontline tools; cycle counts aimed where variance is highest.
InventoryWhat should the system actually trust?A confidence score per SKU × location, built from cycle-count variance.
CapacityWhat can the network realistically fulfil?A live capacity model of pickers, riders and slots by time of day.
Member promiseWhat should we promise the member?A decision engine that combines availability, freshness, ETA and confidence. Try a toy version ↓
DeliveryDid reality match the promise?Promise vs actual logged per order, joined to the inventory and capacity state at the moment of promising.
LearningWhat would I improve once there's real operating data?ETA and spoilage models trained on that history, then agents that handle routine planner decisions once the rules are trusted.

What can we confidently promise the member?

Given inventory, freshness, capacity, distance and operational uncertainty, a promise here is availability + freshness + ETA + confidence. A fast promise is meaningless if the inventory isn't real, the product isn't fresh, or the operation can't fulfil it.

    09 · Member promise

    A promise I can explain, not just display.

    The engine above isn't about being faster. It's about being right. Every promise is built from six inputs, and each one can be traced back. When the system isn't confident, it should know not to over-promise.

    AvailabilityIs it on the shelf?
    FreshnessWill it arrive fresh?
    Inventory confidenceCan we trust the count?
    Picker capacityCan someone pick it now?
    Rider availabilityCan someone carry it now?
    Travel timeHow far is the member?
    A fast promise is meaningless if the inventory isn't real, the product isn't fresh, or the operation can't fulfil it.

    “A fast promise is still a bad promise if the avocado is already guacamole.”

    10 · Delivery

    The floor test

    If I owned this system, I'd validate the model against what actually happens on the floor. Did reality match the promise?

    Metrics I'd use to validate the modelNot FirstClub data · a validation plan

    MetricWhat it tells the modelWhat I'd look for
    Inventory accuracyWhether the trust haircut is set rightVariance by SKU × Club House
    Fill rateHow often we deliver what was orderedShort picks after a confident promise
    Promise adherenceWhether ETAs and buffers are honestWhere misses cluster by node and hour
    Freshness at deliveryWhether the freshness floor holdsAgeing vs member experience
    SubstitutionsGaps the engine didn't see comingRepeat SKU / Club House patterns
    WastageWhether FEFO allocation actually reduces spoilageWrite-offs by lot age
    Pick-pack timeWhether the capacity model is realisticDrift at peak hours
    Throughput per nodeWhere capacity really bindsOrders/hour against the model
    Cost per orderWhether a better promise is also affordableCost of buffers, re-picks, failed deliveries

    The hypothesis I'd test first

    Inventory accuracy may be a bigger driver of promise reliability than the ETA shown on screen suggests.

    11 · Learning

    Give me the shipment. I'll figure out the system.

    Use one shipment to understand the system, find the constraint, and build a more mature operating model.

    ETA · DAY 30

    Understand the system

    Buying → Collection → DC → Middle mile → Club House → Member

    Walk the entire chain. Map:

    • inventory flow
    • information flow
    • decision points
    • ownership
    • operational constraints
    • existing metrics
    • where manual work happens
    ETA · DAY 60

    Find the lever

    Identify the highest-impact constraint. It could be:

    • inventory accuracy
    • replenishment
    • capacity
    • routing
    • freshness
    • picking
    • demand forecasting
    • promise reliability

    Then define one measurable product intervention.

    ETA · DAY 90

    Run the pilot

    Ship one controlled improvement in one node, city or category. Define:

    • baseline
    • success metric
    • experiment
    • operational impact
    • product impact
    • cost impact
    • decision: scale or kill

    I wouldn't arrive with a grand strategy. I'd understand the system, find the constraint, ship something, measure it, and scale what works.
